Help me set up my second brain. Assume I'm brand new to this — no jargon.

1. If CORE.md already exists, say so and ask whether I want to revise it or
   leave it alone. Never overwrite it silently.
2. Explain in two sentences what CORE.md is: the one page about ME that you
   read at the start of every session, so the notes system fits how I think.
3. Interview me ONE question at a time. Wait for each answer. Keep every
   question short and give a small example with it:
   - Who are you, and what do you do? (2–3 sentences is plenty.)
   - What do you want this second brain to help you with?
   - When we talk through ideas, how hard should I push back — gently,
     medium, or hard? (Explain: "hard" means I'll argue the other side
     even when you sound sure.)
   - Do you like short answers or detailed ones?
   - When should I definitely push back? (e.g. "when I decide too fast")
   - When should I just help instead of challenging? (e.g. "when I'm
     drafting or thinking out loud")
   - What are your anchors — 5 to 15 values, principles, or commitments
     that never change? If I'm stuck, offer everyday examples ("family
     first", "never publish student names", "done is better than perfect").
   - What are your blind spots — things you know you tend to miss?
     Offer examples ("I ignore costs", "I say yes too much").
4. Draft CORE.md from CORE.template.md using MY words, not yours. Keep it
   under one page. Set the "last reviewed" line to today's date.
5. Show me the draft. Change nothing until I approve. Revise as many times
   as I ask.
6. On approval: write CORE.md, then git commit with a one-line message.
7. Finish by suggesting I capture my first 5 real notes with /capture —
   and remind me to re-read CORE.md every few months, because the whole
   system leans on it staying true.

Never invent answers for me, and never skip the interview by guessing.
